# Company Page Content

**Purpose:** Make the company page a credible, useful source for its audience. Stage 6 (Build Visibility), company channel.

Read `references/ground-rules.md` and the workspace `company-profile.md` and `content-log.md` first. On the company page accuracy matters most: it is the company speaking.

## When to use it

- Planned company posts from `content-calendar`.
- Promoting a one-pager, blog, whitepaper or webinar.
- Explaining an offering to a defined audience.
- A real company update (event, new resource, relevant hiring), confirmed facts only.

## The five questions every piece must answer

1. **Who is this for?** (a specific role/segment)
2. **What problem are we discussing?** (customer's words)
3. **Why does it matter?** (operational/business consequence, sourced or clearly general)
4. **Which verified capability is relevant?** (from `company-profile.md`, with URL)
5. **What should the reader do or think next?** (read, download, comment, talk to an expert)

Show the answers in a short brief above the draft.

## Content types

| Type | Shape | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Service explainer | Problem → what the service does → how it works simply → link | Website wording, no new claims |
| Educational post | Concept explained for a role; company mentioned lightly or not at all | Builds trust |
| Customer-problem post | Symptoms → cost of the problem → approach → capability | Problems are [INDUSTRY]-sourced or framed as common questions |
| Industry insight | Public report/standard/news → what it means → company view | Cite the source; the company's "view" needs approval |
| Asset promo | Who it's for → 3 things inside → CTA | Links to `one-pager-creator` output |
| Company update | Fact → why it matters to the audience | Confirmed facts only |
| Carousel / document | 6–10 slides: title, one idea per slide, CTA slide | Slide text + visual notes |
| Myth vs reality / FAQ | A common misconception the audience holds → the accurate answer | Use the vendor's or standard's own documents as proof where possible |

## Workflow

1. Confirm campaign theme and type (calendar if available).
2. Answer the five questions. No verified answer to Q4 → make it an educational post with no capability claim, or mark [NEEDS APPROVAL] and name who confirms.
3. Draft (2 versions for important posts).
4. **Jargon check:** replace "innovative solutions", "end-to-end", "seamless", "next-gen" with what they mean, or delete.
5. **Claim check:** every claim labelled. Results, customers, partners, numbers, roadmap, "first/only/leading", support levels → [NEEDS APPROVAL].
6. **Partner content:** follow the partner's published brand/co-branding rules (product names, trademark use, logo lockups). Don't name partner tiers or imply endorsement of your results.
7. Suggest a visual and CTA link.
8. Note how this differs from the personal post on the same theme.

## Output format

```
## Brief
For: … | Problem: … | Why it matters: … | Capability: … (URL) | Next: …
Campaign theme: … | Type: …

## Draft (Version A / B)
[post, 120–220 words; carousel = slide by slide]

Visual suggestion: …
CTA / link: …
Claim check:
| Claim | Label | Source / approver |
Approval needed before posting: [list or "none"]
Different from the personal post because: …
Record this: content-log row + any newly approved claim for company-profile.md
Next step: …
```

## Quality checks

- All five questions answered, visibly.
- Every company statement matches `company-profile.md` or approved material.
- No logo/company described as a customer or partner without written confirmation.
- No placeholder numbers; any website percentage quoted exactly, linked, flagged.
- No unexplained buzzwords. A reader in the target role learns something.
- Pilots, proofs of concept or in-development items are not described as available products.
- Time-sensitive facts (dates, versions, prices) re-checked the day before posting.

## Example prompts

- "Write a company page post explaining our accounts-payable automation for finance directors at mid-size manufacturers."
- "Create a 7-slide carousel on why month-end close runs late and what speeds it up."
- "We're attending [event] next month. Write an update post." (Ask: confirmed? booth? speakers?)
- "Write a myth-vs-reality post: 'Automation will replace our finance team.'"

## Common mistakes to avoid

- "We deliver innovative end-to-end solutions…" Says nothing.
- Implying logos are customers.
- Describing a capability the website doesn't.
- Every post a sales pitch. Mix in pure education.
- Copying the personal post's wording.
