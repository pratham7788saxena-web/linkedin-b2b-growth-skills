# One-Pager & Content Asset Creator

**Purpose:** Package a topic into a simple, shareable asset a busy professional can read in 2 minutes. Supports Stages 6 (Visibility) and 9 (Nurture: a useful thing to share).

Read `references/ground-rules.md` and the workspace `company-profile.md` first.

## When to use it

- A campaign needs a central asset to link from posts and share in conversations.
- A prospect asks "do you have something on X?"
- Before an event, as a leave-behind.

## Information to ask for

| Needed | Why |
|---|---|
| Topic + campaign | Scope |
| Target audience (role, segment) | Language and value focus |
| The customer problem (and confidence) | The heart of the page |
| Approved internal material (deck, approved case studies) | Only source beyond the website |
| Format & where used (LinkedIn document, PDF, web page) | Layout |
| Brand kit (`brand-kit.md`) | Design |

## Workflow

1. **Learn first.** If the topic hasn't been through `topic-learning`, do a short learning summary.
2. **Write the 8 parts:**
   1. **Title**: problem- or outcome-led, plain words (e.g. "Payroll Errors, Caught Before Payday").
   2. **Target audience**: one line, on the page or in notes.
   3. **Customer problem**: 3–4 recognisable symptoms, framed as common challenges [INDUSTRY], not claims about a specific company.
   4. **Simple explanation**: 3–5 sentences or a 3–4 step "how it works".
   5. **Relevant verified capability**: from `company-profile.md` or approved material, source noted.
   6. **Potential business value**: framed as *potential* or *what teams aim for* ("less manual reconciliation", "faster root-cause identification"). Numbers only if approved; website percentages quoted exactly, attributed as anonymised, [NEEDS APPROVAL].
   7. **CTA**: one clear next step that exists (a real page or approved destination).
   8. **Visual structure**: layout blocks, a simple diagram idea, icons.
3. **Claim table:** every statement with its label and approver.
4. **Plain-language pass** for a smart but busy reader.
5. **Produce the file if asked:** PDF/DOCX/PPTX or a design artifact, clearly marked "DRAFT — for internal review". Visuals via `brand-creative-design`.

## Output format

```
# [Title] — One-Pager Draft (for review)
Audience: … | Campaign: … | Use: …

## Page copy
[Headline]
[Sub-headline]
The challenge: • • •
What it is / how it works: 1. 2. 3.
How we help: …
What teams aim for (potential value): • • •
[CTA]

## Suggested visual structure
Block 1 (top): … | Block 2: … | Diagram: … | Footer: …

## Claim check
| Statement | Label | Source | Approval owner |

## Open questions for the internal team
## Record this (content-log asset row + approved claims for company-profile.md)
## Next step
```

## Quality checks

- One page = ~250–400 words of copy.
- A non-specialist understands the problem and the idea.
- No unsupported results; no customers or partners named without approval.
- Value phrased as potential, not guaranteed.
- CTA points to a real destination.
- Marked draft until approved.

## Example prompts

- "Create a one-pager on our expense-management software for finance directors at mid-size retailers."
- "Make a 6-slide LinkedIn document post explaining AI-assisted invoice matching for accounts-payable teams."
- "We need a leave-behind for our managed security offering for CISOs. Draft copy and layout."

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Cramming every feature onto the page.
- "50% cost reduction" without approved evidence.
- Starting with the company instead of the customer's problem.
- Diagrams only engineers can read.
