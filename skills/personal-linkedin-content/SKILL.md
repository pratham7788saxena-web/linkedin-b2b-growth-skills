# Personal LinkedIn Content

**Purpose:** Build the user's personal presence so target audiences recognise their name before they ever message them. Stage 6 (Build Visibility), personal channel.

Read `references/ground-rules.md` first (especially "Accounts and voices"), then `voice-notes.md` and `content-log.md` in the workspace.

## The voice: develop it, don't fake it

- Default for a non-expert: a curious, honest professional who works alongside experts and shares what they learn. Credible because specific and careful, not because of claimed expertise.
- Good formats: "What I learned this week about…", "A question I asked our engineers…", "3 things that surprised me about…", "The simplest explanation I've found of…", event takeaways, a public report summarised with a personal reflection.
- Avoid: pretending to have run programmes they haven't, "As an expert in…", hot takes they can't defend.
- If the user *is* an expert, write from their real experience, and only what they've told you.
- Keep `voice-notes.md` updated with phrases they like, words they avoid, and what worked.

## When to use it

- Planned posts from `content-calendar`.
- After a `topic-learning` session: turn the note into a post.
- After an event, webinar or conversation (with permission if others are named).

## The 5-point brief (ask or infer, then confirm)

1. **Target audience** (a role, not "everyone")
2. **Problem or industry question**, in their words
3. **Main insight**: the one takeaway
4. **Purpose**: educate, start a discussion, share a learning, support a campaign theme
5. **Call to action**: usually a genuine question; rarely "DM me"

If the user seems unsure of the topic, offer a quick `topic-learning` note first.

## Workflow

1. Fill the brief. Check `content-log.md` so the post fits the current theme and doesn't reuse a hook, example or message.
2. Gather substance: a learning note, a public source, or a real experience the user describes. Every fact gets a source.
3. Draft **2 versions** with different hooks/structures (e.g. story-led vs list).
4. Keep the company light: mention it only with a verified capability, and not in every post (roughly 1 in 4).
5. Fact-check note and approvals.
6. Suggest a first comment (e.g. the source link) and 2–3 relevant hashtags.

## Post craft

- **Hook** (first 2 lines, before "…see more"): a specific observation or question, not a cliché.
- **Body:** short paragraphs, one idea each; concrete examples; explain acronyms.
- **Length:** 120–250 words by default.
- **CTA:** a question experts would enjoy answering.
- Natural and professional. No emojis by default; no "Agree?"; no engagement bait.

## Output format

```
## Brief
Audience · Problem/question · Insight · Purpose · CTA · Campaign theme · Source(s)

## Version A — [hook style]
[post]

## Version B — [hook style]
[post]

Suggested first comment: …
Hashtags: …
Fact check: [labels per claim]
Approval needed: … (or "none")
Best time to post: [suggestion; check weekly-review data]
Record this: content-log row (+ hook used)
Next step: …
```

## Quality checks

- Sounds like this person, not a brochure or a different job title.
- An expert reader would find it accurate; simplifications stay correct.
- Audience, problem and insight are visible in the post itself.
- No invented stats, stories or company claims.
- Not a copy of the company-page post on the same theme.

## Example prompts

- "Turn my learning note on data contracts into a personal post for data engineering leads."
- "I attended a webinar on AI governance. Here are my notes — write a post with 3 takeaways."
- "Give me 5 post ideas for this month's campaign that fit my learning-in-public voice."

## Common mistakes to avoid

- "Thought leader" tone the user can't back up.
- Generic "AI is changing everything" posts.
- Every post an advert.
- Tagging people who weren't involved.
- Same message as the company page.
