# Executive Ghostwriting

**Purpose:** Give senior buyers a credible leader to hear from, without putting words in that leader's mouth. Stage 6 (Build Visibility), executive channel.

Read `references/ground-rules.md` first, then `voice-notes-<executive>.md`, `company-profile.md` and `content-log.md`.

## When to use it

- An executive has agreed to post and needs drafts, a voice profile or a content plan.
- A campaign theme should reach senior buyers from a leader's point of view.
- The user wants posts for the executive to comment on or repost.

## Non-negotiable rules

1. **Agreement first.** The executive has agreed to post leadership content. Never assume it.
2. **Written approval for every post.** The executive approves the exact final text and visual in writing (email, chat, team channel). Record the approval in the tracker's Content Calendar. Any change after approval, even one line, needs approval again.
3. **Nothing in their name they haven't said.** No invented stories, opinions, customer conversations or "what I'm hearing from clients" lines. Where their view is needed, insert **[EXEC: …]** for them to fill, or write a clearly opinion-only line with no claimed experience, which they explicitly approve.
4. **Company claims follow `company-profile.md`.** No client names, unapproved numbers or partner tiers.
5. **Replies, DMs and connection requests** come from the executive, or are drafted for them to approve and send themselves. The user doesn't send messages as the executive.
6. **Account access.** LinkedIn's terms ask members not to share logins, and personal profiles have no delegate role. Recommended: the user prepares text and image; the executive posts or schedules it. If the executive chooses otherwise, they should confirm it in writing and understand the risk.
7. **Tag only real people** who agreed to be tagged.
8. **No repeats:** check `content-log.md` for hooks and lines already used on any account.

## Voice capture (do this before the first draft)

- Collect 5–10 of their past posts or articles, and note: sentence length, structure, formatting habits, hashtags, how they open and close, recurring beliefs.
- Ask (or have the user ask) 6–8 questions: what they want to be known for; 3 beliefs about the industry they'd defend; a decision they're proud of; topics off-limits; words they'd never use; who they want to reach.
- Save to `voice-notes-<executive>.md`. Until samples exist, label drafts **provisional voice**: clear, direct, no personal anecdotes.

## Account roles

| Account | Role | Typical angle |
|---|---|---|
| Executive | Strategic point of view; the person senior buyers want to hear from | "Here's how leaders should think about this decision" |
| Company page | Capability and proof | "Here's what we do about it" |
| Personal profile | Learning in public; plain-English explainers | "Here's what I learned" |

One weekly theme can move across all three: different angle, different day, no copy-paste. Reposts always with the executive's own thoughts.

## Workflow

1. Confirm pillars (3–4), e.g. a strategic belief about the industry, an operating philosophy, building the company, a balanced take on a hyped trend. Each pillar names an audience and the campaign it supports. Pillars are proposals until the executive confirms them.
2. Plan cadence with lead time (e.g. 1–2 posts/week; drafts sent by Thursday for the following week; optional monthly article).
3. Draft **2 versions** per post in their voice, with [EXEC: …] gaps where only they can supply the substance.
4. Claim check and approval checklist.
5. Optional: suggest 3–5 posts by target-account leaders for the executive to comment on, with angle ideas they write or approve themselves.

## Output format

```
## Brief
Pillar · Audience · Insight · Campaign link · Voice status (matched / provisional)

## Version A
[post]
## Version B
[post]

[EXEC] gaps to fill: …
Claim check: | Claim | Label | Source / approver |
Approval: needs [executive] written OK of final text + visual
Different from company/personal posts because: …
Record this: content-log row (Account = Executive) + approval note
Next step: …
```

## How we measure it

Comments from relevant roles at target accounts (not total likes), reshares by relevant people, follows/requests from target accounts, inbound DMs, and conversations that enter the tracker with source "executive post". Impressions only if the executive shares analytics.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Inventing a "client conversation last week" for colour.
- Generic CEO motivational posts disconnected from the campaign.
- Publishing an edited version without re-approval.
- The executive and the user both messaging the same prospect.
