# Topic Learning

**Purpose:** Make sure the user understands a topic well enough to market it honestly. Teach first, then suggest angles. Stage 1 (Learn) and the start of Stage 2 (Strategize).

Read `references/ground-rules.md` first, then the workspace `company-profile.md`.

## When to use it

- A campaign starts on a topic the user hasn't marketed before.
- An unfamiliar term appears in a prospect's post, a job title, an RFP or an article.
- Before writing content (`personal-linkedin-content`, `company-page-content`, `one-pager-creator`) on a technical topic.
- When the company's focus shifts to another offering.

## Information to ask for (only what's missing)

| Needed | Why |
|---|---|
| The topic or term | What to teach |
| Why they're learning it now (campaign, prospect, post, curiosity) | Shapes examples and angles |
| Target audience, if known | Which roles to emphasise |
| Internal material on it (deck, notes) | Only source for capabilities beyond the website |
| Depth (5-minute basics vs detailed) | Default: basics + one level deeper |

If the term is ambiguous (e.g. "pipeline" = sales pipeline or data pipeline?), ask one clarifying question.

## Workflow

1. **Check what's known.** Look for `topic-notes/<topic>.md`. Build on it rather than repeating it.
2. **Research when needed.** Anything beyond stable basics: search reliable sources (standards bodies, industry associations, vendor docs, analysts, reputable trade press). For the company side, open the relevant page; don't rely on memory. Note dates.
3. **Teach in layers:** plain English → how it works → a concrete example.
4. **Map to the customer.** What problem does this solve, in the customer's words? Who feels the pain, who decides, who influences?
5. **Map to the company, carefully.** Quote the related offerings from `company-profile.md` with URLs. Separately list where the link is only an [ASSUMPTION]. If the company has nothing verified on the topic, say so plainly. That's a useful finding.
6. **Suggest 3–5 marketing angles**, each tied to an audience and a problem, each labelled *use now / needs approval / needs research*.
7. **Check understanding.** End with 3 self-check questions (answers below them). Offer a quiz.
8. **Offer the campaign brief** if this topic is for a campaign.

## Output format

```
# [Topic] — Learning Note
Checked: [date] · Sources: [list]

## 1. In one sentence
## 2. In simple English (3–5 sentences; an everyday analogy if it helps)
## 3. How it works (4–6 steps or a small text diagram)
## 4. An example (realistic and clearly hypothetical, no real company unless sourced)
## 5. The customer problem (symptoms you'd hear, e.g. "our data never matches reality")
## 6. Who cares inside a buying organisation
| Role | What they care about | Decision-maker / influencer / user |
## 7. Key terms (max 8)
## 8. How this relates to us
**Verified (company-profile / website):** quote + URL
**General industry knowledge:** …
**Assumptions to confirm internally:** … (who could confirm)
## 9. Possible marketing angles
| Angle | Audience | Problem | Channel | Status |
## 10. Check your understanding (3 questions + answers)
## Record this (topic-notes/<topic>.md, campaign-log.md)
## Next step
```

A beginner should finish reading in under 10 minutes.

## Quality checks

- Could a non-specialist explain it to a colleague after reading? If not, simplify.
- Every company statement is quoted from a named page or labelled [ASSUMPTION].
- No invented products, results, customers or partners. Logos are not evidence.
- The example is marked illustrative unless it's a real, sourced case.
- Specific industry facts (dates, standards, levels) have a source.
- Angles name a specific audience and problem, not "raise awareness of X".

## Example prompts

- "Teach me revenue operations (RevOps). We might run a campaign on it next month for mid-size SaaS companies."
- "What is zero-trust network access and does our company do it?"
- "I saw 'FinOps' in a prospect's post. Explain it simply and tell me how it relates to us."

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Jumping to post ideas before the user understands the topic.
- Presenting an industry definition as the company's capability.
- Over-technical explanations full of acronyms.
- Attaching a market buzzword to the company when the website doesn't use it.
- Using anonymised website percentages as general, approved proof.
