# LinkedIn Engagement

**Purpose:** Turn reading into relationships. Thoughtful comments get the user noticed by the right people and teach them the industry. Stage 7 (Engage).

Read `references/ground-rules.md` first.

## When to use it

- Daily engagement block (15–20 minutes).
- `lead-qualification` recommended "comment on a relevant post".
- An industry leader, analyst or prospect posts on a campaign topic.
- Someone comments on the user's or company's posts and a reply is needed.

## Information to ask for

- The post text (pasted) or link, and who posted it (name, role, company if known).
- Is the author a tracked prospect?
- Anything the user knows or has learned about the topic (makes the comment genuine).
- Which account is commenting (user / executive).

Only a link and you can't open it → ask the user to paste the text. Never guess what a post says.

**Quick mode:** during the daily block, or when the user says "quick": a 3-line explanation, the three comments, and one follow-on line. Skip glossary, fact-check table and record block unless something needs flagging. Speed matters: posts get most replies in the first day.

## Workflow

### Step 1: Explain first (for the user, not for posting)
- **What they're saying**: 2–3 plain sentences.
- **Why it matters** to the industry.
- **How it connects** to the user's field and campaign; explain unfamiliar terms.
- **Worth commenting?** Yes/No: relevance, author relevance, freshness (best within 24–48 hours).
- **Risks:** controversial topic, competitor's post, sensitive news (layoffs, outages, incidents) → careful tone or no comment.

### Step 2: Draft three comments
1. **Thoughtful** (40–80 words): pick up one specific point and add a perspective, example or sourced fact, maybe a light question.
2. **Short and natural** (15–35 words): one specific reaction that shows they read it.
3. **Question-based** (when there's a genuine question): specific, answerable, interesting to an expert; not a test or a pitch.

### Step 3: Explain each
One line: what value it adds and which point it builds on.

### Step 4: Follow-on
Follow the author? Save as a content idea (`content-log.md` parking lot)? Log in the tracker if a prospect? Suggest a connection request only if the exchange creates a genuine reason (→ `linkedin-outreach`).

## Comment rules

- Add something: an angle, example, source, honest question, or learning reflection.
- Banned on their own: "Great post", "Very informative", "Completely agree", "Thanks for sharing", emoji-only, "DM me".
- No company promotion by default. Mention the company only if directly asked or clearly helpful, and rarely. Never drop company links in other people's comment sections.
- Don't claim expertise: "From what I've been learning about X…" is fine; "In my 10 years of X…" isn't, unless true.
- Don't correct experts publicly unless sure and sourced. Ask a question instead.
- Vary openings and structure; no template feel.

## Output format

```
## What this post says (for you)
What: … | Why it matters: … | Connection to our topics: … | Terms explained: …
Worth commenting? Yes/No — why | Timing: …

## Comment options
1. Thoughtful: "…"  → adds: …
2. Short: "…"  → adds: …
3. Question: "…"  → adds: …

Fact check: [facts used, with label/source]
Follow-on: …
Record this: tracker activity line (if prospect) / content idea
```

## Quality checks

- The explanation is correct and simple.
- Each comment references a specific point in the post.
- No generic praise, no pitch, no invented facts.
- The question is answerable and interesting.
- Fits LinkedIn comment style (no headings).

## Example prompts

- "Here's a post from a CTO about moving off a legacy platform. Explain it and give me comments."
- "This analyst article says IT budgets are shifting to AI. What does it mean and should I comment?"
- "Someone asked on our company post how we handle data from several ERP systems. Draft a reply." (→ verified wording only)
- "Quick: [pasted post]"

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Commenting without understanding the post.
- "At [Company] we solve this!"
- The same comment template on every post.
- Essays that hijack the author's post.
- Salesy comments on sensitive news.
