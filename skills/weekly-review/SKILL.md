# Weekly Performance Review

**Purpose:** Learn what's working and improve next week's plan. Stage 10 (Track and Improve). Honest numbers beat impressive ones.

Read `references/ground-rules.md` first.

## When to use it

- Every Friday (or Monday for the previous week).
- End of a campaign (retrospective: same structure, longer period).

## Information to ask for (actual data only)

| Group | Metrics | Where the user finds them |
|---|---|---|
| **Activity** (what we did) | Posts by account, comments made, accounts researched, people identified, requests sent, messages sent | Tracker Activity Log, calendar |
| **Engagement** (how people reacted) | Impressions, reactions, comments, reposts, engagement rate; **relevant engagement** = from target roles/accounts; profile views; followers gained | LinkedIn post and page analytics |
| **Lead** (relationships moving) | Acceptance rate, replies, reply rate, conversations started, qualified opportunities | Tracker stage counts |
| **Business results** | Meetings proposed, meetings booked, opportunities handed to sales | Tracker, sales |

Missing metric → list under "Not provided"; never estimate. Inconsistent numbers (more accepts than requests) → point it out and ask.

## Workflow

1. **Organise** numbers into the four groups. Compare with last week and the plan's targets.
2. **Calculate** simple rates only from provided numbers, with the formula: acceptance = accepted ÷ sent; reply = replies ÷ messages; engagement = engagements ÷ impressions.
3. **Quality, not just volume:** which posts drew target roles/accounts? Which hooks, topics, formats? Which messages got replies? Compare accounts (personal vs company vs executive) if relevant.
4. **Careful conclusions:** one week and small numbers show signals, not proof. Say so. Look for patterns over 3–4 weeks.
5. **Write the review.**
6. **Update records:** `campaign-log.md` lessons, tracker Weekly Metrics row, `voice-notes.md` "what worked"; hand improvements to `content-calendar`.

## Output format

```
# Weekly Review — [Campaign] — week of [date]

## Scorecard
| Group | Metric | This week | Last week | Target | Note |
(Activity / Engagement / Lead / Business — separate blocks)
Not provided: …

## What worked (with evidence)
## What did not work (with evidence)
## What the data cannot tell us (yet)
## Three practical improvements for next week
1. [specific change] — why — how we'll know it worked
2. …
3. …
## Record this (campaign-log + Weekly Metrics row)
## Next step: plan next week in content-calendar with these changes
```

Offer a simple trend chart once there are 3+ weeks of data.

## Quality checks

- Every number comes from the user's data; calculations shown.
- Four groups kept separate; impressions aren't presented as business results.
- Conclusions match sample size; uncertainty stated.
- Improvements are specific, doable next week, measurable.
- No blame language. It's a learning loop.

## Example prompts

- "Weekly review: 3 personal posts (impressions 820, 1,240, 410), 2 company posts, 14 requests sent, 8 accepted, 2 replies, 0 meetings. Last week: 10 sent, 4 accepted."
- "Here's my tracker export and an analytics screenshot. Review the week."
- "Campaign retrospective for the 6-week campaign."

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Filling gaps with guesses or "industry averages".
- Celebrating impressions while conversations are zero, or panicking after one week.
- Changing everything at once. Change 2–3 things so you can tell what helped.
- Counting connections as leads.

## How it connects to the next skill

The three improvements go into `content-calendar`. Lessons about topics or audiences may trigger `topic-learning` or `target-account-research`. The loop starts again.
